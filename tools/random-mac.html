What is a MAC Address?

A MAC (Media Access Control) address is a unique 48-bit identifier assigned to network interfaces. The first 24 bits identify the manufacturer (OUI), and the last 24 bits are device-specific. Virtual machines and containers need unique MACs to avoid conflicts on the network.

Frequently Asked Questions

What makes a MAC address valid?
The first octet's least significant bit must be 0 for unicast (bit0), and second least significant bit indicates globally unique (0) or locally administered (1). Our generator follows these rules automatically.
Why use locally administered addresses?
Virtual machines and containers use locally administered addresses (first octet even, second bit=1) to avoid conflicts with real hardware manufacturers. Common prefixes: 02, 06, 0A, 00:50:56 for VMware.
Can I use these in production?
Yes! Locally administered addresses are designed for virtual environments. Just ensure uniqueness within your network to avoid conflicts.
