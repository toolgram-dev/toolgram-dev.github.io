What is HIIT Calorie Burn?

HIIT (High-Intensity Interval Training) alternates short bursts of intense exercise with recovery periods. This training style burns significant calories during the workout and creates an "afterburn" effect (EPOC) that elevates metabolism for hours after exercise. HIIT typically burns 8-15 METs depending on intensity, making it one of the most time-efficient calorie-burning exercises.

This calculator estimates calorie burn based on HIIT type and intensity, including the afterburn effect. HIIT provides unique benefits: maximum calorie burn in minimum time, improved cardiovascular fitness, increased VO2 max, preserved muscle mass during fat loss, and time efficiency. A 20-minute HIIT session can burn as many calories as 45-60 minutes of steady-state cardio.

HIIT Training Tips

  • Start Gradually: If new to HIIT, start with moderate intensity and shorter durations (10-15 minutes). Build fitness gradually to prevent injury and overtraining.
  • Proper Warm-Up: Always warm up for 5-10 minutes before HIIT. HIIT places high demands on the body - warming up prevents injury and improves performance.
  • Work-to-Rest Ratio: Common ratios include 1:1 (30 sec work, 30 sec rest), 2:1 (40 sec work, 20 sec rest), or Tabata (20 sec work, 10 sec rest). Adjust based on fitness level.
  • Push Hard During Work: During work intervals, give maximum effort. The intensity during work periods determines HIIT effectiveness. You should be breathless by the end of each interval.
  • Recovery is Key: Use rest periods for actual recovery. Active recovery (light movement) is often better than complete rest for maintaining heart rate elevation.
  • Limit Frequency: HIIT is demanding - limit to 2-3 sessions per week with rest days between. More is not better and can lead to overtraining and injury.
  • Listen to Body: HIIT should be challenging but not painful. Distinguish between discomfort (normal) and pain (warning sign). Stop if experiencing dizziness or chest pain.
  • Combine with Other Training: HIIT works best when combined with steady-state cardio and strength training. This provides comprehensive fitness while preventing overuse injuries.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories does HIIT burn?
HIIT burns approximately 10-15 calories per minute depending on intensity. For a 70kg person, moderate HIIT burns about 10-11 calories/min, high-intensity 12-13, extreme 14-15. A 20-minute session typically burns 200-300 calories plus 50-100 additional calories from afterburn effects.
What is the HIIT afterburn effect?
The afterburn effect (EPOC - Excess Post-exercise Oxygen Consumption) is elevated calorie burn after exercise as the body recovers. HIIT creates significant afterburn, potentially adding 50-150 calories over several hours post-workout depending on intensity.
Is HIIT better than steady cardio for weight loss?
HIIT is more time-efficient and creates greater afterburn, but steady cardio can be performed longer and may be more sustainable. Both are effective - the best approach combines both for variety and comprehensive benefits.
How long should HIIT sessions be?
Effective HIIT sessions typically last 15-30 minutes. Quality matters more than quantity - 20 minutes of true high-intensity intervals is more effective than 60 minutes of moderate effort. Tabata is as short as 4 minutes.
Can beginners do HIIT?
Yes, but beginners should start with modified HIIT - lower intensity, longer rest periods, and shorter durations. Build base fitness with steady cardio before attempting true high-intensity intervals. Consider consulting a fitness professional.
How often should I do HIIT?
Most people benefit from 2-3 HIIT sessions per week with at least one rest day between sessions. HIIT is taxing on the body - more frequent training increases injury risk and can lead to overtraining. Quality over quantity.
