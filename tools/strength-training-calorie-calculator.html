What is Strength Training Calorie Burn?

Strength training, also known as resistance training or weightlifting, burns calories through both the immediate energy expenditure during exercise and the long-term increase in resting metabolic rate from muscle gain. Calorie burn during strength training varies based on weight lifted, intensity, rest periods, and duration - typically 3-6 METs depending on intensity.

This calculator estimates calorie burn based on training type and intensity. Strength training provides unique benefits beyond calorie burn: increased muscle mass, improved bone density, enhanced metabolism, better functional strength, and improved body composition. The afterburn effect (EPOC) from strength training can elevate metabolism for hours after the workout.

Strength Training Tips

  • Progressive Overload: Gradually increase weight, reps, or sets over time. This is the key to building strength and muscle. Track your progress to ensure consistent progression.
  • Compound Movements: Focus on compound exercises like squats, deadlifts, bench press, and rows. These work multiple muscle groups and are more efficient than isolation exercises.
  • Proper Form First: Learn correct technique before increasing weight. Poor form causes injury and reduces effectiveness. Consider working with a trainer initially.
  • Rest Between Sets: Allow adequate rest between sets (1-3 minutes for strength, 30-90 seconds for hypertrophy). Rest is essential for performance and recovery.
  • Train All Muscle Groups: Balance pushing and pulling movements. Include upper and lower body exercises. Imbalances lead to poor posture and injury risk.
  • Warm Up Properly: Always warm up before lifting with 5-10 minutes of light cardio and dynamic stretching. This prepares muscles and prevents injury.
  • Allow Recovery: Muscles grow during rest, not training. Allow 48-72 hours between training the same muscle group. Sleep is crucial for recovery.
  • Combine with Cardio: For optimal fitness and health, combine strength training with cardiovascular exercise. This provides comprehensive fitness benefits.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories does strength training burn?
Strength training burns approximately 4-8 calories per minute depending on intensity. For a 70kg person, moderate training burns about 5-6 calories/min, high-intensity 7-8 calories/min. A 60-minute session typically burns 250-450 calories, plus additional afterburn effects.
Does strength training boost metabolism?
Yes, strength training significantly boosts metabolism both short-term through the afterburn effect (EPOC) and long-term by increasing muscle mass. Muscle tissue burns more calories at rest than fat tissue, raising your BMR permanently.
Is strength training better than cardio for weight loss?
Both are valuable. Cardio burns more calories during the workout. Strength training builds muscle, which increases resting metabolism 24/7. The best approach combines both for optimal weight loss and body composition.
How often should I strength train?
Beginners should start with 2-3 sessions per week. Intermediate trainees can handle 3-4 sessions. Advanced lifters may train 4-6 days but must split routines to allow muscle group recovery. Never train the same muscle group on consecutive days.
Will strength training make me bulky?
Not unless you specifically train for bulk with high calories. Most people, especially women, will develop a lean, toned appearance from strength training. Building significant muscle mass requires dedicated training, high calorie intake, and often genetics.
What are the benefits beyond calorie burn?
Strength training offers numerous benefits: increased bone density, improved joint health, better posture, enhanced functional strength for daily activities, reduced injury risk, improved insulin sensitivity, and better mental health. These benefits often outweigh calorie considerations.
