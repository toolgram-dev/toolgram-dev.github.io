What is State Income Tax?

State income tax is levied by individual states on income earned within that state. Rates and structures vary dramatically - from 0% in nine states (Alaska, Florida, Nevada, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, Wyoming, New Hampshire) to over 13% in California for high earners. State taxes are deductible on federal returns (subject to $10,000 SALT cap), making them a key factor in relocation decisions.

This calculator helps you understand your state tax burden based on your residence, income level, and filing status. For remote workers or those considering relocation, comparing state taxes can reveal significant savings opportunities. Remember to consider total tax burden including property and sales taxes, not just income tax.

FAQ

Which states have no income tax?
Alaska, Florida, Nevada, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, Wyoming, and New Hampshire (only interest/dividends taxed in NH). These states fund government through sales tax, property tax, and other revenue sources.
How do state tax brackets work?
Most states use progressive brackets (like federal) where higher income is taxed at higher rates. Some states (Illinois, Pennsylvania, Massachusetts) use flat tax where all income is taxed at the same rate. California has the most progressive system with 9 brackets.
Can I deduct state taxes on my federal return?
State income tax is deductible as part of SALT (State and Local Tax) deduction, but capped at $10,000 per household. This limit affects high earners in high-tax states most significantly.
Do I pay tax where I work or live?
Generally, you pay tax to your resident state on all income. If you work in a different state, you may owe tax there too, but your resident state typically gives a credit for taxes paid to other states. Some neighboring states have reciprocity agreements to simplify this.
Which states tax retirement income?
Most states don't tax Social Security. Many exempt pension income (Illinois, Pennsylvania, Mississippi, Alabama, Hawaii). States with no income tax don't tax any retirement income. Check specific state rules as they vary significantly.
What is tax reciprocity?
Reciprocity agreements allow workers who live in one state but work in another to pay tax only to their resident state. Common examples include DC metro area agreements and Midwest state compacts. Eliminates double taxation and simplifies filing.
What's the most tax-friendly state?
Depends on your situation. No-tax states are best for high earners. States like Florida, Tennessee, and Pennsylvania are popular with retirees due to no tax on Social Security/pensions. Consider total burden: income + property + sales taxes.
